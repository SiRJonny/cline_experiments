# Modern weight-loss drugs (Wegovy / Mounjaro / Saxenda) while trying to conceive and in early pregnancy

**Scope:** semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy/Rybelsus), tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), liraglutide (Saxenda/Victoza) and, where relevant, exenatide. Compiled 2026-10-28 from the sources listed at the end (drug labels, specialty-society guidance, human cohort studies, RCTs in PCOS, animal/reproductive-toxicology data). Everything below is limited to what those documents state.

---

## 1. The short answer

| Question | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| **Can I take these while trying to conceive?** | Yes, but with a planned stop. ACOG (Oct 2026): "consider discontinuation **1–2 months before conception**" [1]. The Wegovy label: discontinue **at least 2 months** before a planned pregnancy because of the long half-life [2]. Some reviews suggest **4 weeks** [5], others **8–10 weeks** for semaglutide/tirzepatide [8]. The Endocrine Society/ESE guideline suggests **stopping before conception** rather than mid-first-trimester in type 2 diabetes [7]. |
| **Can I take them in pregnancy?** | **No.** ACOG "should advise **against** the use of GLP-1RAs during pregnancy" [1]; labels state human data are insufficient, animal data show fetal harm, and weight loss offers no benefit in pregnancy [2,3]. |
| **Is there proof they cause birth defects in humans?** | **No signal of a large increase so far**, but data are observational, small and confounded — the largest study (938 exposed infants, all with type 2 diabetes) found **no increased risk of major malformations** vs insulin (aRR 0.95, 95% CI 0.72–1.26) [4]. Absence of evidence, not evidence of safety. |
| **Do they help fertility?** | In **PCOS with obesity**, weight loss from GLP-1RAs/metformin **increases spontaneous pregnancy rates** in small RCTs [9–11], which is why unplanned pregnancies occur. ACOG notes "some studies indicate improved spontaneous and assisted fertility, which also increases exposure risk" [1]. No trial has tested them as a fertility drug in unexplained infertility. |
| **Are there real trials in pregnancy?** | **None.** No prospective trial of these drugs in pregnancy exists in the retrieved literature [1,6]. A pregnancy exposure registry exists for Wegovy [2]. |

---

## 2. What the drugs are and why the question is urgent

- GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs): semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, exenatide. Tirzepatide is a **dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist** [15].
- All delay gastric emptying and reduce appetite; semaglutide and tirzepatide have **long half-lives**, which ACOG identifies as the reason that "avoidance of medication during pregnancy" is difficult [1].
- **Use is expanding in exactly the age group that conceives:** ACOG reports that GLP-1RA prescribing in the perinatal period "increased more than **thirtyfold between 2019 and 2024**", that 12% of US adults had ever used them, and that about **one in five** current users obtained them through an online clinician or medical spa [1].
- Measured exposure in pregnancy is small but rising: **32–295 per 100,000 pregnancies** across Nordic, US and Israeli cohorts (increasing over time) [4]; **32 of 104,422** singleton pregnancies in one Danish region [13].

## 3. Regulatory (label) positions — the most concrete guidance that exists

### Wegovy (semaglutide) — current US label, version dated 30 June 2026 [2]

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **8.3 Females and Males of Reproductive Potential** | "For patients receiving WEGOVY for CV risk reduction or weight reduction, or for MASH where the potential risk outweighs the potential benefit, **discontinue WEGOVY at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy** because of the long half-life of semaglutide." |
| **8.1 Pregnancy** | Available pharmacovigilance and trial data "are **insufficient to establish a drug-associated risk** of major birth defects, miscarriage or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes." **"Weight loss offers no benefit to a pregnant patient and may cause fetal harm."** Discontinue in pregnant patients using it for weight reduction. |
| **Animal data (8.1)** | Rats: **embryofetal mortality, structural abnormalities and growth alterations** at **clinically relevant** maternal exposures (at the maximum recommended human dose, by AUC). Rabbits and monkeys: **early pregnancy losses and structural abnormalities** at clinically relevant exposures — "these findings coincided with a marked maternal body weight loss in both animal species." |
| **Registry** | A **pregnancy exposure registry** exists (Novo Nordisk, 1-877-390-2760; wegovypregnancyregistry.com). |
| **8.2 Lactation** | "Breastfeeding is **not recommended** during treatment with WEGOVY tablets" (SNAC excipient present in milk; semaglutide itself below quantification in a tablet lactation study). |

### Mounjaro (tirzepatide) — current US label, version dated 2 September 2026 [3]

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **8.1 Pregnancy** | "Available data with MOUNJARO use in pregnant women are **insufficient** to evaluate for a drug-related risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes… **should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus**." |
| **Animal data** | Rats: **fetal growth reductions and external, visceral and skeletal malformations** at clinical exposure, which "coincided with pharmacologically-mediated reductions in maternal body weights and food consumption." Rabbits: fetal growth reductions at clinically relevant exposures. |
| **8.3 / Drug interactions** | "Use of MOUNJARO **may reduce the efficacy of oral hormonal contraceptives** due to delayed gastric emptying… advise patients using oral hormonal contraceptives to **switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after initiation and for 4 weeks after each dose escalation**." |
| **Note** | Unlike the Wegovy label, this Mounjaro label version gives **no explicit "discontinue X months before planned pregnancy"** instruction — only the contraception advice. |

**Why the labels say "2 months" but the trials cannot prove safety:** the animal findings occurred at *clinically relevant* exposures and were coupled with maternal weight loss — i.e. they may reflect the pharmacology of under-nutrition rather than direct teratogenicity [2,3]. Human placental transfer appears low: exendin-4 was not transported across the placenta; exenatide fetal:maternal ratio ≤0.017 in one ex vivo placenta; liraglutide showed no significant transfer at ≥3.5 h in a single subject [12].

## 4. What professional bodies say (2023–2026)

| Body / document | Position |
|---|---|
| **ACOG Practice Advisory (October 2026)** [1] | **Prepregnancy:** advise about the lack of sufficient safety data and **consider discontinuation 1–2 months before conception**; encourage evidence-based lifestyle intervention and weight surveillance after stopping. **Pregnancy:** **advise against use**. **Lactation:** shared decision-making *may* include GLP-1RAs; if used, close surveillance of **neonatal weight gain**; **recommend against** use with preterm infants/neonates. Also: because weight loss may improve fertility and increase conception likelihood, "patients who can become pregnant should be counselled regarding **effective contraception** while using these medications." Notes that **exenatide would be preferred** if a GLP-1RA were indicated, because it has the shortest half-life. |
| **Endocrine Society + European Society of Endocrinology, Preexisting Diabetes and Pregnancy (2025)** [7] | In type 2 diabetes, "we **suggest discontinuation of GLP-1RA before conception** rather than discontinuation between the start of pregnancy and the end of the first trimester" (Grade 2, low-quality evidence). |
| **EASO position statement (2025)** [14] | "Obesity can negatively impact fertility; **weight loss of 5–10% over 6 months improves fertility**." "In women with PCOS, treatment with **metformin and GLP-1 receptor agonists or surgery can be considered**." "There is **limited clinical data** on the safety and efficacy of obesity medication during pregnancy or lactation." |
| **Narrative reviews in fertility journals (2023–2026)** [5,8,15] | Minis et al.: human data too sparse to set a washout; "a **4-week** washout period prior to attempting conception may be considered." Becker et al. (2026): "semaglutide and tirzepatide require an **8–10 week washout** period prior to conception"; tirzepatide may reduce oral contraceptive effectiveness. Duah & Seifer (2025): GLP-1RAs "can be considered for use in the preconception period, as long as they are **stopped at least 2 months before conception**." |

## 5. What the human pregnancy data actually show

There are **no randomized trials**. Everything is observational, mostly in women with type 2 diabetes, and all of it is vulnerable to confounding (women on these drugs have higher BMI, more diabetes, more comorbidity).

### 5.1 Major congenital malformations — the largest study [4]

**Cesta et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2024** — population-based cohorts from 4 Nordic countries, US MarketScan and Israeli Maccabi (2009–2021); infants of mothers with type 2 diabetes; exposure = ≥1 prescription fill from 90 days before pregnancy to end of first trimester; comparator = insulin.

| Outcome | GLP-1RA-exposed | Insulin-exposed | Adjusted RR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any major congenital malformation | **75/938 (8.2%)** | 7.8% | **0.95 (0.72–1.26)** |
| Cardiac malformations | **23/938 (3.2%)** | 4.2% | **0.68 (0.42–1.12)** |
| First-trimester-restricted exposure only | — | — | **1.03 (0.73–1.47)** |

Authors' conclusion: results "did **not** indicate a large increased risk of MCMs above the risk conferred by maternal T2D requiring second-line treatment," but estimates were imprecise and confirmation is needed. **Important limitation raised by the AJOG review [6]:** there was no information on maternal glycaemic control, and diabetic embryopathy depends on hyperglycaemia itself — so the comparison cannot fully separate drug from glucose control.

### 5.2 Semaglutide specifically — the Danish cohort [13]

104,422 singleton pregnancies (Central Denmark Region, conceptions 2016–2023). **32 exposed to semaglutide in the first trimester** (27 Ozempic — 22 of them also on insulin — and 5 Wegovy); 17 stopped in the first trimester, **14 continued throughout all trimesters**.

| Outcome | Semaglutide (n=32) | Insulin (n=547) | No diabetes drug (n=103,843) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major malformations | 3/32 (9.4%) | 76/547 (13.9%) | 7985/103,843 (7.7%) | 
| — semaglutide vs insulin | OR 0.64 (0.12–2.15), p=0.78 | | |
| — semaglutide vs unexposed | OR 1.24 (0.24–4.01), p=0.49 | | |
| Preterm birth | 20.0% | 23.1% | 4.8% |
| Gestational age at birth (days) | 265 | 263 | 279 |

Interpretation by the authors: **no increased risk of major malformations**, and the preterm/LGA/hypoglycaemia/jaundice risks were **similar to insulin-exposed** pregnancies but higher than unexposed. **The numbers are tiny (32 exposed, 3 malformations)** — this is reassurance, not proof.

### 5.3 Discontinuing around conception — the "catch-up" signal [16]

**Maya et al., JAMA 2025** — 149,790 singleton pregnancies in one US health system; GLP-1RA order between 3 years before and 90 days after conception; 448 exposed pregnancies propensity-matched to 1,344 unexposed.

| Outcome | GLP-1RA-exposed | Unexposed | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gestational weight gain | 13.7 kg | 10.5 kg | **+3.3 kg (95% CI 2.3–4.2)** |
| Excess gestational weight gain | 65% | 49% | **RR 1.32 (1.19–1.47)** |
| Preterm delivery | 17% | 13% | **RR 1.34 (1.06–1.69)** |
| Gestational diabetes | 20% | 15% | **RR 1.30 (1.01–1.68)** |
| Hypertensive disorders | 46% | 36% | **RR 1.29 (1.12–1.49)** |
| SGA, LGA, birth length, caesarean | no difference | no difference | — |

The authors' reading: in a cohort composed mainly of women with obesity, **stopping the drug shortly before or during early pregnancy was associated with more gestational weight gain and higher risks of preterm delivery, gestational diabetes and hypertensive disorders**. This is a confounding-prone association (drug use marks severe obesity), but it reframes the counselling: the *withdrawal* period is itself a risk window that needs weight surveillance — exactly what ACOG recommends [1].

### 5.4 Systematic review of offspring effects [12]

39 records (9 theoretical, **7 human**, 23 animal; 76 human offspring in total). Animal findings dominate: **reduced fetal weight and/or growth, delayed ossification, skeletal variants**, usually with reduced maternal weight gain and food intake. Human placental transfer data are minimal (see §3). Conclusion: the authors "found evidence for adverse offspring effects… also in human studies" and support **discontinuing during pregnancy and lactation** — but note that this conclusion rests on very few human data points.

## 6. The fertility side: actual randomized trials (all in PCOS, not in unexplained infertility)

These are the trials that explain both the enthusiasm **and** the exposure risk. None was designed to test pregnancy safety; pregnancy is a secondary/observational outcome.

| Trial | Population & design | Key results |
|---|---|---|
| **Li et al. 2022** (Arch Gynecol Obstet) [9] — long-term follow-up of an RCT | 160 overweight/obese Chinese women with PCOS and infertility; **exenatide vs metformin for 12 weeks**, then metformin alone until pregnancy; ART offered if not pregnant by week 64 | **Spontaneous pregnancy at week 24: 29.2% vs 14.7% (p=0.03)**; total pregnancy by week 64: 79.2% vs 76% (p=0.65, ns); **no significant difference in pregnancy outcomes**; spontaneous pregnancy associated with weight loss and HOMA-IR improvement |
| **Chen et al. 2025** (Reprod Biol Endocrinol) [10] — prospective RCT, open-label | 100 overweight/obese women with PCOS; **metformin 1000 mg BID vs metformin + semaglutide 1 mg weekly for 16 weeks**; all on metformin weeks 16–40 | Weight loss **−6.09 ± 3.34 kg vs −2.25 ± 4.27 kg** (p<0.01); greater improvement in testosterone, CVAI and CRP; higher rate of **menstrual cycle recovery**; **natural pregnancy 35% vs 15% (p<0.05)** |
| **Elkind-Hirsch et al. 2022** (Fertil Steril) [11] — phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled | 82 women with obesity and PCOS; **liraglutide 3 mg vs placebo for 32 weeks** + lifestyle | Weight **−5.7% vs −1.4%**; more participants achieved ≥5% loss (25/44 vs 5/23); free androgen index fell; **GI events 58.2% vs 18.5%** (mostly mild–moderate) |
| **Becker et al. 2026** (JBRA Assist Reprod) [8] — narrative review of 47 articles (2014–2025) | Summary of the above and other studies | "Clinical trials reporting increased **menstrual regularity, ovulation and pregnancy rates** — particularly when combined with metformin"; one RCT: pregnancy **69.2% with liraglutide + metformin vs 35.4% with metformin alone (p<0.05)**; ovulation rates up to **86%** with exenatide + metformin |

**Mechanistic/endometrial data (relevant if you are having fertility treatment):**
- GLP-1 receptors are expressed in reproductive tissues; proposed effects include anti-inflammatory, anti-fibrotic and androgen-lowering actions [8].
- Liraglutide induced autophagy and dose-dependent growth inhibition in an endometrial cancer cell line; exenatide reduced endometrial degeneration and fibrosis in diabetic rats — interpreted by the authors as possible **anti-inflammatory/anti-fibrotic** benefit for an endometrium affected by obesity, diabetes or PCOS. But the same review states plainly: "the effects of GLP-1RAs on the **endometrium remain unclear**" and calls for clinical studies [17].
- A cautionary review argues there are **no preclinical data on the ovarian implications of semaglutide**, that GLP-1 signalling intersects insulin/IGF-1/mTOR/sirtuin pathways relevant to oocyte competence, and that **off-label semaglutide use should be avoided** until its effects across the reproductive axis are studied [18].

## 7. What is genuinely not known

1. **No prospective human trial** of semaglutide or tirzepatide in pregnancy — ACOG: "both the quantity and quality of human studies of GLP-1RAs are limited" [1].
2. **Miscarriage risk** cannot be quantified; the AJOG review notes "little evidence available regarding fetal growth restriction, embryonic or fetal death, or other potential complications" [6].
3. **Tirzepatide human pregnancy data are essentially absent** — the label says data are insufficient [3]; no cohort equivalent to the semaglutide Danish series was found.
4. **Long-term child outcomes** (neurodevelopment, metabolic programming) are unstudied in humans; animal studies show offspring metabolic effects in mice when semaglutide was given *before* pregnancy [19].
5. **Fertility-treatment outcomes** (IVF live birth) have not been tested; a 2021 Cochrane review "found insufficient evidence to recommend pharmacologic treatment for women with obesity and infertility" [1].
6. **Endometrial receptivity/implantation** effects are unknown in humans [17].
7. **Optimal washout duration** is not evidence-based; recommendations range 4 weeks to 2 months/8–10 weeks and are extrapolated from half-life [1,2,5,8].

## 8. Practical implications if you are trying to conceive

**Decide the plan before starting the drug, not after a positive test.** The consistent thread across every source is that the risk is managed by *planning*, not by testing after the fact.

1. **If you are not trying this month:** use effective contraception. ACOG explicitly recommends counselling about contraception because the drugs may restore fertility [1]; the AJOG review makes the same recommendation [6]. With **tirzepatide**, avoid relying on oral contraceptives — switch to non-oral or add a barrier for **4 weeks after starting and 4 weeks after each dose increase** [3].
2. **When you want to conceive:** stop the drug **1–2 months before** trying (ACOG) [1]; **at least 2 months** for Wegovy per its label [2]; **8–10 weeks** has been proposed for semaglutide/tirzepatide [8]; **4 weeks** is the minimum proposed by one review for the older agents [5]. The Endocrine Society prefers stopping **before** conception over stopping in early pregnancy [7]. Plan the stop with your clinician so that the weight-loss phase ends before the conception window opens.
3. **Do not stop and immediately start IUI/IVF the same cycle without a plan.** ACOG recommends "evidence-based lifestyle interventions and **weight surveillance** after discontinuing GLP-1RAs" [1] — and the JAMA cohort shows that women who stop close to conception gain about **3.3 kg more** in pregnancy, with higher rates of excess gain, preterm birth, gestational diabetes and hypertensive disorders [16].
4. **If a pregnancy occurs while you are still taking the drug:** stop it and contact your clinician promptly. ACOG: weight loss "offers no benefit to a pregnant patient" and the drug should be discontinued in pregnancy used for weight reduction [2]; the human data so far show **no pattern of malformations** but are too thin to promise safety [4,6,13]. Consider enrolling in the **Wegovy pregnancy registry** [2].
5. **Do not continue through pregnancy for weight control.** ACOG advises against GLP-1RA use in pregnancy [1]; EASO notes limited safety/efficacy data in pregnancy and lactation [14]; the Endocrine Society suggests stopping before conception in type 2 diabetes [7].
6. **Breastfeeding:** ACOG allows shared decision-making, with **close monitoring of infant weight gain**, and recommends against use with **preterm** infants [1]; the Wegovy label does not recommend breastfeeding with the **tablet** formulation [2]; LactMed states tirzepatide is usually **undetectable in milk** at doses up to 5 mg and is "not a reason to discontinue breastfeeding," while advising caution with a newborn or preterm infant [20].
7. **If you have PCOS and obesity,** this is where the drugs have the best (still modest) fertility evidence: GLP-1RAs ± metformin increased spontaneous pregnancy rates in small RCTs, alongside 5–10% weight loss [9–11,14].
8. **If your infertility is unexplained and you are not obese,** nothing in these sources supports using a GLP-1RA as a fertility treatment: the Cochrane review found insufficient evidence for pharmacologic obesity treatment in infertility [1], and a 2025 review warns against off-label use of semaglutide in fertility care until ovarian effects are characterised [18].

---

## 9. Bottom line

- **These are genuinely effective weight-loss drugs with a plausible fertility benefit in obesity/PCOS — and that benefit is precisely what creates pregnancy exposure.**
- **No trial has ever tested them in pregnancy, and none is likely to.** The evidence is limited to observational cohorts, animal reproductive toxicology, and small PCOS RCTs with pregnancy as a secondary outcome.
- **The human signal so far is reassuring for major malformations** (adjusted RR ≈ 0.95 vs insulin in 938 exposed infants; 3/32 malformations in the Danish semaglutide cohort), **but it cannot exclude moderate risks**, and it says nothing about miscarriage, long-term child outcomes, or IVF outcomes.
- **Every authoritative body converges on the same practical rule: stop before conception** (1–2 months per ACOG; ≥2 months per the Wegovy label; 8–10 weeks proposed by one review), **do not use in pregnancy, and use contraception if pregnancy is not intended** — with tirzepatide requiring extra contraceptive caution.
- **The under-appreciated point:** the months *after* stopping are a risk period of their own — more gestational weight gain and more preterm birth, gestational diabetes and hypertension in women who stopped near conception [16].
